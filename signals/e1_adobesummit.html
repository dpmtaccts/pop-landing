Adobe Summit 2026 Takeaways.

What Adobe announced at Summit and what it means for your stack strategy. A short briefing with the highlights, plus a quick read on each theme below.

Chris DresslerChief Digital Officer, POP12 min watch · 6 min read
Watch the short version MP4 Download the Transcript TXT

Summit didn't disappoint this year. Big bets on AI as expected, a clearer agentic posture, and a portfolio-wide MCP push that's quietly the most consequential story of the week.

Here are the five themes that stood out — and what to do about each.

01 The big picture

Adobe is the orchestration layer, not the foundation model.

Adobe crystallized its AI strategy at Summit. They aren't building frontier models—they're orchestrating them.

The expanded partner list now spans AWS, Anthropic, Google Cloud, IBM, Microsoft, NVIDIA, and OpenAI—with everything riding on open standards (MCP and agent-to-agent). The Experience Cloud rebrand to CX Enterprise signals the bigger shift: it's not a stack of point products anymore, it's an orchestration platform that runs agents across the customer lifecycle.

Key takeaways
  1. 01Think orchestration, not point tools. Treat CX Enterprise as the connective tissue across your existing Adobe investments.
  2. 02Keep LLM flexibility on purpose. You don't have to commit to one vendor—confirm the LLMs you trust fit cleanly through MCP and A2A.
  3. 03Make MCP and A2A non-negotiable for new buys. Most stacks need a light retrofit to be ready.
  4. 04Set up an early proof point. Audience-to-activation is a good starting place.
02 Agentic CX

CX Enterprise & Co-worker: set a goal, approve a plan, watch agents execute.

The headline product at Summit. A conversational interface that orchestrates content, audiences, channels, and measurement end-to-end.

Give it a prompt like "lift cross-sell by 3% in my outdoor wear category," and it assembles audiences, pulls the most aligned creative, drafts offers, and recommends channels. A human approves the plan, then agents execute against it. It's built on Engagement Intelligence, Journey Optimizer, CX Analytics, and the real-time CDP. The biggest shift: non-technical users can describe an outcome and have the platform orchestrate it.

Key takeaways
  1. 01GA is months, not weeks. Use the runway to prepare, not to wait.
  2. 02Pick low-risk jobs first. Cross-sell, win-back, loyalty, audience expansion. High-stakes creative stays human-led for now.
  3. 03Rewire the operating model in parallel. Who approves agent-drafted plans, who owns measurement, where humans stay in the loop.
  4. 04Audit data and system readiness early. Coworker needs structured, connected data.
03 Content authority & LLM optimization

Your next biggest channel doesn't click, it cites.

Adobe now sees brand visibility inside LLMs as its own discipline—and they have two products carrying the load.

LLM Optimizer tracks how often your brand shows up across Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity—measuring visibility score, share of voice, and sentiment. Its opportunities dashboard flags missing summaries, schema gaps, and crawlability issues, then deploys fixes either back to AEM or at the CDN edge (serving optimized HTML to AI agents without touching what humans or SEO bots see). Brand Intelligence sits on top of AEM as a continuously learning context layer that keeps agent-generated content on-brand.

Key takeaways
  1. 01Treat GEO like a real channel. Resource it the way you'd have resourced SEO five years ago.
  2. 02Run an LLM visibility audit first. Find gaps, factual errors, and structured-data holes before turning anything on.
  3. 03Use AEM as your brand context layer. Governed, machine-readable content is how you stay visible to agents.
  4. 04Approach owned, earned, and social differently. Owned needs structure. Earned needs the outlets LLMs over-index on. Social needs authoritative engagement.
04 AEM Guides update

Structured content for the LLM era.

Under-the-radar at Summit, but arguably the most LLM-ready content platform Adobe already ships.

Two big updates. First, a new MCP server for AEM Guides—writers and engineers can interact with topics, maps, branches, and publishing workflows from inside Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor, or Copilot. Second, Editor 2.0, a fully modernized authoring experience that looks like Microsoft Word and removes the need to wrangle raw DITA XML, with AI tooling sprinkled in.

The bigger point: LLMs do their best work when inputs are structured, semantic, and trustworthy. That's literally what AEM Guides delivers—single-sourced atomic topics, semantic markup, and governed workflows. In the same Summit where Adobe pushed LLM Optimizer and AEM-as-context-layer, Guides quietly became the connective tissue.

Key takeaways
  1. 01Get Editor 2.0 / MCP in front of your teams. Productivity and AI-assisted drafting both improve once authors are out of raw-XML mode.
  2. 02Use LLM discoverability to justify the investment. "Agents need structured content" is the cleanest funding argument you'll have this year.
  3. 03Migrate content out of unstructured systems. Word, SharePoint, and homegrown wikis won't survive the agentic era.
  4. 04De-risk the DITA learning curve. Migration anxiety is the most common reason teams delay.
05 MCPs everywhere

Adobe just made it dramatically faster to build with and on top of its products.

One of the most undercover themes at Summit—and one of the most consequential for delivery teams.

For engineering: AEM now ships MCP servers covering content (read/write) and Cloud Manager. Engineers describe code changes or components in natural language to their agent of choice; scaffolding that used to take hours happens in minutes.

For marketers: The Adobe Marketing Agent is GA in Microsoft 365 Copilot and in beta in Claude Enterprise, ChatGPT Enterprise, and Gemini Enterprise. Pull Adobe data and trigger workflows from inside tools your teams already use.

For content ops: Workfront added a Workflow Optimization Agent. GenStudio for Performance Marketing added support for ChatGPT Ads.

Key takeaways
  1. 01Pilot AEM MCP with one engineering pod. Measure cycle-time on a real backlog. The reaction drives adoption faster than any slide.
  2. 02Bring Adobe data into tools your teams already use. Less context-switching, more done.
  3. 03Audit custom integrations for MCP/A2A readiness. Anything custom-built can likely be simplified or replaced.
  4. 04Plan for MCP across the portfolio. This is just the start—Adobe is rolling MCP into more products over the coming year.
